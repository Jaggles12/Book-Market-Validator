What actually went wrong (in human words)
Nothing is wrong with Rainforest or the idea; the bug is in your pipeline logic. Amazon returned 18 very on-target domestic/psychological thrillers with strong BSRs. Your enrichment step correctly pulled ranks like 27, 29, 53, 650, 29k. Then your relevance step took those books, ran an OpenAI similarity pass, and decided that every single one was “out of niche” because the final scores were below your hard thresholds. Since you only calculate stats and market signal from “core + adjacent” books, the moment that bucket is empty, your stats builder thinks there are zero relevant books, zero competition, zero demand—and wipes the book list. So the “no demand / no competition” outcome is entirely self-inflicted: strict filtering + no fallback when filters nuke everything.

Streamlined, generalizable fix (one paragraph)
Keep the architecture, but make it more forgiving and keyword-aware instead of genre-specific hacks: (a) add one OpenAI “keyword normalizer” step that turns the user’s idea into one canonical Amazon genre label + a primary search term + 2–3 fallback search terms (e.g., canonicalGenre: “Domestic Thriller”, primary: “domestic thriller suburban”, fallbacks: “domestic thriller”, “psychological domestic thriller”); (b) if your first Rainforest call returns books but your relevance pass ends up with zero core/adjacent comps, automatically do a soft fallback: reuse the same books, treat the top N by semantic score as “adjacent” instead of throwing them away, and compute stats from that set instead of returning zeros; and (c) make this fallback logic universal—it doesn’t care if the idea is fiction or nonfiction, it only kicks in when “we clearly have books + ranks, but our strict filter would otherwise show ‘no data’.” That gives you robust behavior across all genres without a pile of special-case tweaks: one keyword-normalization call in, one graceful “never show 0 when Amazon is screaming ‘crowded market’” rule in the middle, and the rest of your pipeline stays the same.
